Chromosome Yearbook

Every person has 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs. You get one chromosome in each pair from each biological parent. Point at a picture to see what that chromosome does. On a tablet, tap it.

ChromosomeA tiny bundle in your cells that holds your DNA.
GeneOne instruction on a chromosome. It tells your body how to build something.
TraitSomething about you, like eye color or blood type.
Color key:

The colored lines on each chromosome show where the gene sits. Pictures are drawn to scale, so big chromosomes look big.

Remember: Most traits, like height and skin color, come from many genes working together.

Chromosome 9 = blood type. Chromosome 15 = eye color. X = color vision. Y = helps guide how the body develops.
